Mathematical Formula

How Loan EMI Is Calculated

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender at a specified date each calendar month. EMIs apply to both interest and principal each month so that over a specified number of years, the loan is fully paid off.

In India, banks and housing finance corporations (HFCs) calculate loan EMIs using the standard reducing-balance annuity formula:

EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n - 1]
  • P (Principal): The total loan amount borrowed from the bank.
  • r (Monthly Interest Rate): The annual interest rate divided by 12 months and divided by 100 (e.g. 8.5% annual rate = 8.5 / 1200 = 0.0070833).
  • n (Tenure in Months): The loan repayment period in years multiplied by 12 (e.g. 20 years = 240 months).

Step-by-Step Worked Example (Using Initial Values)

Let us calculate the EMI for a Home Loan of ₹25,00,000 at an interest rate of 8.5% p.a. for a tenure of 20 years (240 months):

  1. Monthly interest rate: r = 8.5 / 12 / 100 = 0.00708333
  2. Compound factor: (1 + r)^240 = (1.00708333)^240 ≈ 5.409395
  3. Numerator: 25,00,000 × 0.00708333 × 5.409395 ≈ 95,790.62
  4. Denominator: 5.409395 - 1 = 4.409395
  5. EMI: 95,790.62 / 4.409395 = ₹21,695.59 (rounded to nearest rupee: ₹21,696/month)
  6. Total Repayment over 20 years: 21,695.59 × 240 = ₹52,06,939
  7. Total Interest Paid: ₹52,06,939 - ₹25,00,000 = ₹27,06,939

Loan EMI & Repayment FAQs

On floating-rate home loans, when the benchmark rate (such as RBI Repo Rate) increases or decreases, lenders typically keep your monthly EMI constant and adjust your remaining tenure. However, you can also request the bank to keep the tenure unchanged and recalculate your monthly EMI amount instead.
Even small prepayments made early in your loan tenure can save you lakhs of rupees in interest because prepayments directly reduce the principal balance. For example, paying just one extra EMI per year on a 20-year home loan can reduce your overall tenure by over 3 to 4 years.
In a flat rate calculation, interest is charged on the full principal amount throughout the tenure, which leads to a significantly higher effective interest rate. In a reducing balance calculation (the standard used by reputable Indian banks), interest is charged only on the outstanding principal balance each month.
